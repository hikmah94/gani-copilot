# Roadmap: from Niger State to Nigeria and Africa

The roadmap prioritises trust, local usefulness, and operational sustainability over rapid geographic expansion. Items below are planned, not claims about the current deployment.

## Phase 1 — Judge-ready Niger State proof of concept

- Reliable search-to-evidence journey.
- Nine document-backed annual budgets.
- Structured project and allocation exploration.
- Controlled AI tools and document-scoped retrieval.
- Moderated community observations.
- Public limitations and evidence-quality labels.

## Phase 2 — Reliable Niger State civic service

- Complete and continuously monitored Vectorize indexing.
- Cloudflare Queues and Workflows for resilient ingestion.
- AI Gateway policies, caching, cost controls, and evaluations.
- Turnstile, strengthened WAF rules, abuse monitoring, backups, and incident response.
- Hausa-language testing and low-bandwidth/offline-friendly views.
- Civil-society review partnerships and correction workflows.
- Procurement, releases, expenditure, and audit records where officially available.

## Phase 3 — Multi-state Nigeria platform

- Configurable jurisdiction and fiscal-year schemas.
- Source adapters for state budget offices and open-data portals.
- Alias packs for local MDAs, sectors, LGAs, and terminology.
- Comparative views that preserve differences in accounting structure.
- SMS, WhatsApp, and voice access evaluated with privacy and misinformation safeguards.
- State-specific moderation and data-stewardship partnerships.

## Phase 4 — African civic-record infrastructure

- Country and subnational data models developed with local practitioners.
- Multilingual retrieval evaluated by native speakers.
- Regional evidence and provenance standards that remain compatible with local law.
- Reusable open-source ingestion and verification components.
- Federated deployments so organisations can govern their own data and moderation.

## Success measures

- Percentage of material answers with independently openable evidence.
- Accuracy of deterministic calculations against source records.
- Search success and time-to-source on low-end mobile devices.
- Correction turnaround time and indexing freshness.
- User comprehension across languages and literacy levels.
- Share of community observations reviewed without compromising reporter safety.

Expansion should proceed only when a jurisdiction has reliable source access, local terminology review, responsible data stewardship, and partners able to handle corrections and community safety.
